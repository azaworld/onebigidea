Sharks Are Older Than Trees

The earliest shark-like scales in the fossil record date to about 450 million years ago, while the first trees appeared around 385 million years ago. For tens of millions of years, sharks swam in oceans next to land with no forests at all. Modern shark groups came much later, but the shark lineage beat trees by a long way.

The idea in 60 seconds

Sharks are older than trees.

The first shark-like scales show up in rocks from about 450 million years ago.

Back then, land was almost bare. Only tiny, low plants clung near the water.

The first trees did not appear until around 385 million years ago.

That means sharks cruised the oceans for about 65 million years before a single forest existed.

To be fair, today's shark families are younger. But the shark line itself came first.

What other ancient creature do you think is older than you would guess?
