ANCHORING BIAS 🏷️ The Black Friday Price Trick on Your Brain

November 27, 2026 · 48-second video · 3 action points · quiz
YouTubeInstagramFacebook
The idea in one line

The first number you see becomes an anchor, so judge a deal by its final price, not the crossed-out one.

Try this today

Check yourself

With five seconds to estimate, which group guessed higher: 8x7x6x...x1 or 1x2x3x...x8?

The lesson

Two groups estimated the exact same math problem. One guessed over four times higher. Blame anchoring bias.

Psychologists Tversky and Kahneman gave students five seconds to estimate eight times seven, down to one. Others saw one times two, up to eight.

Their median guess was 2,250, versus 512. The real answer? 40,320.

Black Friday prices work the same way. Nielsen Norman Group explains that a crossed-out original price anchors what an item feels worth.
